AS PAINTING

Guidance

A little practical guidance for people planning painting work in their home or property.

Quality

A paint job is not only about price. Preparation, suitable materials, careful application and the way a home is treated all affect how the finished work looks and lasts.

Your home

Residential painting is personal. People may be living, working or caring for family while work is happening. Good communication, dignity and respect for the space matter.

Colour & finish

You do not need to know every paint term before you call. Explain the room, how it is used and the result you want. Colour, sheen and durability can then be discussed in practical terms.

Preparation

A clean result starts before the first coat. Surfaces need to be sound, clean and dry; filling, sanding and the correct primer can make a major difference to adhesion and finish.

Where DIY painting often goes wrong

  • Preparation is rushed. Loose paint, dust, grease, cracks or filler marks are painted over.
  • Primer is skipped or the wrong one is used. Bare, porous, stained or difficult surfaces may need a particular system.
  • Sanding is missed. Some previously painted surfaces need a key, and some finishes benefit from light sanding between coats.
  • The brush or roller is overloaded. Too much paint can mean runs, heavy texture and slower drying; more paint is not automatically a better coat.
  • Recoating is rushed. The previous coat needs the drying time specified for the product.
  • The wrong paint is chosen. Bathrooms, kitchens, exterior masonry, wood and metal can have very different requirements.

Professional painting is not about making the job mysterious. It is about doing the preparation and product selection that the finished surface depends on.
